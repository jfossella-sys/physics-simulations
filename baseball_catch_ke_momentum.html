⚾ How can a pitch that moves 10x faster have 10x momentum and 100x kinetic energy?

Momentum (mv) and kinetic energy (½mv²) are often thought of as the same thing, especially when it comes to fast-moving objects. One key difference is that momentum is a vector, while kinetic energy is a scalar (just a number). Another is the way they change with respect to TIME versus DISTANCE.

Adjust your pitch velocity and also the resistance of the catchers hand as it moves back over time and distance. Notice how momentum and kinetic energy dissipate differently with respect to time and distance.

How are momentum and work connected through power? Force changes the ball’s momentum over time (F = |Δp|/Δt). That same force removes kinetic energy by doing work over distance (|ΔKE| = F·Δx). Power = Force × velocity (P = F·v) connects these ideas: velocity tells us how much distance the ball travels each second, so multiplying force by velocity tells us how quickly the force removes energy. At the start of the catch the ball is fast, so energy is removed rapidly. As it slows, the same stopping force removes the same amount of momentum each second, but less energy each second. That is why the momentum curve is straight versus TIME while the energy curve bends versus TIME—and why energy is straight versus DISTANCE while momentum bends versus DISTANCE.

Don’t feel bad if this is still hard to grasp ... Some of the greatest scientific minds struggled with this very question more than 300 years ago! Beginning in 1686, Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz challenged ideas associated with René Descartes, sparking a debate that lasted for nearly a century! Scientists argued over whether motion was best understood through mass × velocity (momentum) or mass × velocity squared (a precursor to kinetic energy). This became known as the vis viva controversy, meaning “living force.” Eventually, scientists recognized that both quantities matter, but describe different aspects of motion. Momentum helps us understand how force changes motion over TIME, while kinetic energy helps us understand the work done as an object moves through DISTANCE. So if these graphs make you think twice, you’re in good company: you’re exploring a question that helped shape modern physics!
